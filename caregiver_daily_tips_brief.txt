# HealHer - Caregiver "How you can help her today": daily tips library (DRAFT v0.2 for clinician review)

**Nothing here is approved.** 86 tips: 16 prenatal only, 14 postnatal only, 56 for both stages. A prenatal caregiver draws from 72 tips and a postnatal caregiver from 70. The app shows **five a day**, picked at random.

## 0. What changed in v0.2

Version 0.1 had 55 practical tips. To keep the daily list fresh, v0.2 adds **31 lighter tips** in three new types, and a daily mix so that every day has some variety:

- **Ask her** (quick check-in questions, for example "How are you feeling today, really?")
- **A little kindness** (small gestures, for example a favourite drink, a kind note, a song she loves)
- **Try together** (short things to do side by side, for example a slow-breathing minute, a daily chart, a meal)

Each tip also carries a short label for its type (table below), which the app can show as a small tag so the list does not look the same every day.

## 1. What "validated" means here (please read first)

- The **practical tips** come from advice that health services and clinical charities publish for partners and families: Ireland's HSE, NSW Health, the Queensland Government, the Australian Government's pregnancy information service, Raising Children Network, PANDA, Tommy's (reviewed by a midwife) and the WHO Thinking Healthy Programme manual. Where possible each is also backed by research on partner support [S8].
- **The new light tips are different.** The principle behind each one is sourced (listening without rushing, noticing progress, sharing good times, encouraging small steps, a mood and rest chart, slow breathing). **The exact question or gesture is ours.** They are labelled *Principle-based (confirm)*, and a clinician must approve them one by one.
- **No trial tested any individual tip.** The research shows that partner emotional closeness, communication and practical support protect against low mood and anxiety [S8], and that family-involved programmes help in some trials [S9].
- **Evidence labels:** *Health-service guidance* = published advice from at least one health body. *Guidance + research* = guidance plus a research source. *Principle-based (confirm)* = the principle is sourced, the wording is ours. *Derived (confirm)* = our wording based on risk-factor research, with no direct advice source.
- **All sources are from Ireland, Australia, the UK and WHO.** None speaks to Indian family life: joint families, mothers-in-law, visitors and customs after birth, or pressure about the baby's sex. Local clinicians should review the tips and add their own.
- Most sources speak to **partners**. Applying them to mothers, mothers-in-law or other relatives is our assumption, flagged on some tips.
- The app never shows scores, severity labels or the word "depression" to a caregiver. No tip mentions medication.

## 2. How the daily five works (plain code, no AI)

1. If the caregiver's result is **"Get help today"**, show **no tips**, only the support screen with verified helplines (Tele-MANAS 14416, Women Helpline 181, 112).
2. Use only tips for the woman's stage plus the "both" tips. Only **approved** tips are shown (drafts only in development).
3. **The daily mix:** one *Ask her* question, one *little kindness* or *try together* tip, one *do* tip, one *say* tip, and **a wildcard**. A seeded coin makes the wildcard either a "notice", "avoid", "for you" or "when to get help" tip, or any type.
4. Limits: at most one each of notice, avoid, "for you" and "when to get help"; at most two "say" and two light tips; and no two tips on the same main topic.
5. Fixed for the day: the shuffle uses the caregiver, the stage and the local date, so the same five show all day and change at midnight.
6. **Variety:** tips shown in the last seven days are used last. If a tip has to repeat, the one shown longest ago goes first.
7. **Result-aware nudge:** "Talk to her doctor soon" always includes a tip about appointments or starting a conversation. "Extra support would help" favours tips that match flagged areas (money, tension, isolation, baby, support). Both mappings are proposals.
8. Only tip ids and the date shown are stored. Never the caregiver's answers.

**Tested** (reference code, 90-day simulation per stage): always five different tips; every day has a check-in, a light tip, a "do" and a "say"; the limits hold; a prenatal woman never gets a postnatal tip and the reverse; the same caregiver and day give the same five; different caregivers get different lists; and no tip returned sooner than six days after it was shown. About 70 of the 72 prenatal and 69 of the 70 postnatal tips were shown in 90 days. A check-in question can come back after about a week, because there are only eight or nine per stage. That seems acceptable for a daily check-in, but you may want more.

## 6. Gaps and decisions for the clinician

1. Approve, edit or remove every tip. Review the flagged ones first: *Principle-based* and *Derived* tips, the sensitive loss and history tip (CG_P_HISTORY), the head and foot rub (needs consent and a cultural check), the partner-only tips, and the night-feed tip.
2. Add tips for Indian family settings (in-laws, visitors, customs after birth), written with local advisers. Check that gestures such as gifts, flowers or a rub suit the family's culture.
3. Confirm the mapping from questionnaire flags to favoured topics, the seven-day variety window, and five tips a day.
4. The Royal College of Psychiatrists carers page and the NICE guideline could not be read, and the full list of 214 expert-consensus recommendations [S10] was not available. Only the first part of the WHO manual was read (sessions 10 onward were not), so more family-related content may exist.
5. Hindi, Bengali and Tamil text needs a proper translation process. The fields are empty. The type labels need translating too.
6. The tips use "her". Decide whether the wording should also allow for non-birthing parents or other family members.
7. One idea for later: a one-line "why this helps" under each tip. It would make the list more engaging but adds a review and translation load.
