# HealHer: personalised suggestions for the mood reflection (DRAFT v0.1 for clinician review)

Prepared 31 Oct 2026. Companion file: `sentiment_suggestions.json`. **Nothing here is approved for users.**

## 1. Why this change
The old library had about 12 generic suggestions, so most reflections got the same tips. The new library has 70 draft items. Each is tied to a theme the writer mentioned (sleep, feeding, loneliness and so on), the pregnancy stage, and the tone of the note. Code picks the items; the model only reads the note and returns tone and themes, as before.

## 2. How an item is chosen
1. No tips at all for a distressed tone, risk language or any escalation. Those users see only the support block (verified helplines and the safe text).
2. Candidates must match the stage, the tone and at least one detected theme. Generic items are a last resort, at most one per result.
3. A result has 3 items: one that normalises ("many mothers feel this"), plus up to two actions or ways to get help. At most one "see a clinician" item.
4. Items shown in the last 14 days are skipped when other choices exist. Only item ids are stored, never note text.
5. A tired user gets low-effort items first. A low-mood user gets a small-step action.
6. Each tip says "Because you wrote about ...", using fixed labels, not model text.

## 3. What the research supports (and how strongly)
| Theme | What I found | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Sleep | Disrupted sleep goes with about 3.7x risk of low mood in pregnancy and 2.7x after birth. CBT-I is first-line for perinatal insomnia; an RCT of a 5-week adapted programme lowered symptoms at 6 months after birth. Sleep restriction should be adapted in pregnancy | Moderate. I read a summary blog and one review; open the primary papers |
| Anxiety and baby-health worry | Meta-review of 24 reviews: CBT and mindfulness-based therapy strongest; mind-body (yoga, relaxation) positive, better when led by a trained instructor; Cochrane found some but not strong evidence | Moderate |
| Fear of childbirth | Psychoeducation lowered fear (Hedges g 0.59, 6 studies); a second meta-analysis agrees but its reported CI looks wrong | Moderate. Check the CI and the odd cesarean odds ratio in the full text |
| Feeding | Pain and unmet expectations link to worse mood; help with feeding linked to lower later risk; pressure to breastfeed raises anxiety. Mostly observational | Weak to moderate. Sensitive wording |
| Baby crying | Parent programme taught that it is acceptable to lay a fed, changed, well baby down and step away. Authors say crying education and parent mental health need more weight | Programme content only |
| Bonding | Kangaroo care raised bonding and lowered mothers' low mood, but **only in preterm or low-birth-weight babies** | Moderate for that group; extrapolation for others |
| Loneliness and peer support | Loneliness is central to perinatal low mood (27 studies). Peer support cut postpartum low-mood incidence (OR 0.69, 7 studies) | Qualitative plus meta-analysis |
| Family support | Women describe a gap between hoped-for and real support | Qualitative |
| Self-criticism and guilt | Self-compassion correlates negatively with postpartum low mood (9 studies, correlation only, conference proceeding). Fear of being a "bad mother" is a common theme | Weak |
| Money | Money stress is a risk factor; **no intervention evidence found** | Risk factor only |
| Work and return to work | Supervisor and colleague support linked with lower stress and lower risk; longer leave linked with better health. Only 3 mental-health studies | Weak |
| Low mood, any theme | Behavioural activation (Thinking Healthy; Healthy Activity Programme, adults in India); CBT and IPT first-line (ACOG CPG 5); digital CBT lowers postpartum low mood and anxiety (31 RCTs, SMD -0.64 and -0.49) | Strongest tier |
| Tiredness | ACOG lists medical checks (thyroid, blood count, iron, B12). The fatigue-intervention meta-analysis was blocked | Guideline for checks only |
| Nausea | ACOG PB 153: early treatment prevents complications. Ginger evidence limited. Medicines not mentioned by the app | Guideline (2015; check for a newer one) |

## 4. Gaps you should know about
- **15 of 70 items have no source yet** (`needs_source = true`). The app cannot approve them until you supply one or delete them. They are mostly "this is common" sentences for tiredness, body symptoms, recovery, pregnancy symptoms, work and positive moments.
- **No evidence found or opened for:** postpartum perineal and caesarean pain care (the guideline page would not open), postpartum fatigue interventions (PMC blocked), partner involvement, a positive-moments or gratitude practice, and recovery after a traumatic birth.
- **Thin coverage:** prenatal feeding worries, prenatal bonding, and postnatal fear of childbirth have no items. These fall back to tone-level items.
- **Secondary sources:** sleep, peer support, breastfeeding and digital health findings came from summary pages, not the papers. Open the papers before approval.
- **India:** I found no India-specific evidence for peer groups, workplace rights or money schemes. Items that mention local help are flagged `verify_local`, and no scheme is named.
- **Not used:** medication, supplements, ginger, herbal products, acupuncture (evidence limited or outside the app's scope).

## 5. Decisions for the clinician
1. Approve, edit or delete each item. Delete or source the 15 `needs_source` items.
2. Skin-to-skin (BON_SKIN): keep for all babies, or only when the baby was early or small?
3. Feeding items (FED_HELP, FED_CHOICE): confirm wording suits your service's stance on breastfeeding.
4. CRY_BREAK ("lay baby safely in the cot and step away"): confirm this is the message you want, and the safe-sleep wording to add.
5. Check TIR_CHECK, BOD_TELL, BON_TELL and SCG_CBT against the ACOG pages (flag `verify_page`).
6. Is storing the ids of shown suggestions per user acceptable for privacy? It lets us avoid repeats; it can be switched off.
7. Review Hindi, Bengali and Tamil text after translation.
